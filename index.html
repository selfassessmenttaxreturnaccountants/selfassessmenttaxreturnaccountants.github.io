Self Assessment for Cleaners and Cleaning Firms: Rounds, Supplies and Agency Work

Cleaning income looks simple — houses, offices, end-of-tenancy jobs — until the return asks which work was yours, which was an agency's, and which costs belong to which round.

Your own rounds

Payments from your own clients are income in full. Keep the round book or app records per client and per visit; the weekly pattern is the evidence if HMRC ever asks how the figures were built.

Agency and subcontract work

If an agency pays you, that pay is still self-employed income unless the agency puts you on payroll — check the payslip for PAYE deductions before assuming. Work you pass to other cleaners, and what you pay them, belongs on the return too, with their invoices kept.

Supplies and equipment

Chemicals, cloths and consumables are day-to-day costs. Machines — carpet cleaners, buffers, a work vehicle — are equipment: bought outright they may qualify for capital allowances; hired, the hire charges are a running cost. Products supplied to clients and recharged are income when recharged, not a wash.

Travel between jobs

Mileage between clients' premises is claimable; the drive from home to the first job of a regular round usually is not. A simple mileage log per round settles the question the return asks every year.

The deadline

File online and pay by 31 January 2027 for 2025/26. Weekly records through the year turn the return into a summary instead of a reconstruction.
